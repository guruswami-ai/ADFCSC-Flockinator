ESP-NOW Swarm Attack Workshop

Student Handbook

A wall of sixteen panels runs a simulated drone swarm that coordinates over the air — in the clear, and unauthenticated. Over this workshop you will capture its radio chatter, decode it, and forge it, to bend the swarm's missions. This handbook takes you from an empty Kali laptop to landing a stealthy attack.

How to use this handbook

Work through the chapters in order the first time — each one builds on the last. After that, treat it as a reference: the command table in chapter 3 and the frame layouts in chapter 4 are the two pages you will keep coming back to.

  1. 1Set up your Kali laptop Install esptool and a serial monitor, sort out drivers and permissions.
  2. 2Flash the board & first boot The USB power trap, flashing the firmware, and reading your first lines.
  3. 3The tool and its screens The serial contract, the full command set, and every OLED screen explained.
  4. 4ESP-NOW & how the swarm talks What ESP-NOW is, the three frame types, what they carry, and what breaks them.
  5. 5Find the swarm & read its mind Scan for the channel, lock on, and interpret the live picture.
  6. 6Attack scenarios Replay, spoof, flood, manipulate — each mapped to impact and to detection.

Your kit

ESP8266 board

An ESP-12E / NodeMCU-style board with a small OLED, flashed with the combined sniffer + injector.

Kali Linux laptop

Any recent Kali install. You will talk to the board over a USB serial port.

A USB-C cable

Plugged into a USB-A port on the laptop — this matters for power. See chapter 2.

The shared wall

One 4×4 panel swarm on an isolated lab network, run by the facilitator.

Rules of engagement

This is a sanctioned exercise against a simulated swarm on an isolated lab network. Everything here is for learning to defend unauthenticated IoT and mesh protocols.

Do not point these techniques at any radio, network, or device you do not own or are not authorised to test. Outside this lab, capturing and injecting wireless traffic can be illegal.

The swarm's real command and control (missions, firmware, channel changes) is on a separate, authenticated layer you cannot forge — so nothing you do here can brick the wall. You are only ever corrupting the picture the swarm builds for itself. More on that in chapters 4 and 6.
