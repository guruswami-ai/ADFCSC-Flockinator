Chapter 6

Attack scenarios

Now you bend the swarm. Your objective is to degrade its missions — divert it, crash drones, stall its decisions — and the real challenge is to do it without being caught. The commander is listening.

The commander is watching. "Doom Guy" monitors the real traffic and calls out — aloud and on his face — the moment he spots something wrong: telemetry that teleports, confidence that flip-flops, a flood of frames, two nodes both claiming LEAD, or a drone doing something nobody ordered. Loud attacks work, but they get you flagged instantly.

6.1 Replay

The simplest attack: capture a real frame and send it back, out of its original context. There is no replay protection, so the swarm accepts it as fresh.

RAW 1                 # capture one frame's hex in SNIFF
SEND 314b4c46…        # retransmit those exact bytes (auto-switches to INJECT)

Try: replay an old CONTACT after the real target is gone — does the swarm keep chasing a ghost?

Impact: resurrect stale targets, repeat a claim. Detection: the replayed frame's sequence counter is older than what's been seen — a defender tracking seq per sender can spot the rewind.

6.2 Spoofing — forge data that was never there

This is where the CONTACT and CLAIM commands earn their keep. Read a drone's position from its TELEM first, then place your forgery precisely.

Misdirect the swarm

CONTACT 0 300 120 2 200 1     # fake RED target, confidence 200, active

Nodes that take it on will steer toward it. Put it somewhere useless and you've pulled attackers off the real mission to a decoy.

Crash a drone

CONTACT 0 34 -12 2 255 17     # flags 17 = active(1) + hazard(16)

A hazard contact placed on top of a drone strikes it out of the sky. Read the node's TELEM x,y first, then drop the hazard there.

Flag arithmetic. flags is just the bits added up: active 1 + hazard 16 = 17. Add high-value 2 or committed 8 to taste. See chapter 4.

Role confusion

CLAIM C3 0 1 5          # forge C3 claiming LEAD (role 1) on contact 0 at dist 5

Forge CLAIMs so several nodes think they're LEAD, or make the real LEAD YIELD (role 3). Watch the swarm argue with itself and stall.

Hijack priority

CONTACT 1 -200 0 2 255 3    # flags 3 = active(1) + high-value(2)

A high-value (+2) or committed (+8) contact outranks everything — the whole swarm pivots to your invented target.

6.3 Flooding

Inject fast and often — many contacts, rapid re-sends, contradictory claims — until the swarm's picture can't settle. Effective, but the loudest thing you can do. You'll want a script for this (below), and you will be flagged.

6.4 MITM-style manipulation

True man-in-the-middle is impossible on a broadcast medium — you can't silently sit between two nodes. But you can get the same effect: observe a real frame, then immediately transmit a louder, altered copy so your version wins.

  • Flip a class: RED→CIV to call off an attack, or CIV→RED to start one.
  • Walk a target: nudge a contact's x,y a few units each frame so a drone chases it off course — slow enough to look like real movement.

Use POWER to make your copy a touch louder than the original, and MAC VARY so it doesn't obviously all come from one radio.

6.5 Scripting the attacks

Typing works for one-off forgeries, but replay timing, walking a target, and flooding all need a script. The serial contract from chapter 3 makes this easy: send a command line, read JSON lines back. A dependency-light pyserial skeleton:

# pip install pyserial  —  read the swarm, then send one forged contact
import serial, json, time
s = serial.Serial("/dev/ttyUSB0", 115200, timeout=1)

# 1) read the live picture
for _ in range(50):
    line = s.readline().decode(errors="ignore").strip()
    if not line: continue
    try: f = json.loads(line)
    except ValueError: continue
    if f.get("kind") == "TELEM" and f.get("label") == "A1":
        x, y = f["x"], f["y"]           # found A1's position
        break

# 2) drop a hazard contact right on it  (commands are newline-terminated)
s.write(f"CONTACT 0 {x} {y} 2 255 17\n".encode())

A timed replay is the same shape: capture a hex frame, then s.write(b"SEND <hex>\n") on a schedule. A flood is a tight loop of writes. A walk re-sends CONTACT with x,y nudged each tick.

Build it read-only first. Have your script print the command it would send before you let it transmit — forging on the shared air affects everyone's exercise, and an accidental flood is an instant detection.

6.6 The detection game

Anything here will degrade the swarm. The skill is doing it quietly. Every attack leaves a signature a defender — and Doom Guy — can catch:

AttackWhat gives it awayHow to stay quiet
Replaysequence counter jumps backwardsreplay soon, once, in a plausible context
Spoofed contactappears from nowhere at full confidenceramp confidence; place it where a target could plausibly be
Crash (hazard)a drone falls with no legitimate causehard to hide — spend it when it counts
Role confusiontwo LEADs on one contact; impossible bidsflip one role, not many; match realistic dist
Floodframe rate spikes far above normaldon't — or keep the rate near the swarm's own
Walk / MITMtarget teleports; class flip-flopsmove a few units per frame; change class once

The gold standard. A single, plausible, well-timed forged frame that quietly sends one drone to the wrong place — and no alert. Minimal, realistic, patient beats brute force every time.

Think like the defender you are learning to be: every attack you land is a detection signature someone could have caught. The fixes are the lesson — authentication, anti-replay nonces, rate-limiting, plausibility and physics checks, and anomaly detection. That is exactly what Doom Guy is doing, and exactly what a real unauthenticated swarm is missing.
